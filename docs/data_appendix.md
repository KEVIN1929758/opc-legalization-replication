# Data Appendix — Legal Sources for the OPC Legalization Events

*Companion to Table 1. Each entry gives the legal instrument, the operative provision creating or permitting the single-member company, the effective date and its source, and the statutory entry cost used in the §4.3 barrier analysis. Verification tier: **[P]** primary source (statute text, official gazette, regulator's own publication); **[S]** secondary source (law-firm briefing, reputable press); **[N]** not established.*

**Entry-cost measure.** For each reform we record the statutory minimum capital required of the single-member form *as enacted*, in local currency, and divide it by GNI per capita in the same currency and the same year (World Bank indicator `NY.GNP.PCAP.CN`, retrieved 7 August 2026). Expressing the requirement as a multiple of income per capita follows Djankov et al. (2002) and Klapper, Laeven and Rajan (2006) and avoids exchange-rate conversion entirely, since numerator and denominator are both same-year local currency. Non-capital statutory restrictions (minimum director counts, turnover floors or ceilings, mandatory conversion thresholds, notarial requirements, one-per-person rules) are recorded separately and enter the robustness classification in §4.3.

## Ghana — Companies Act, 2019 (Act 992)

- **Operative provision.** s.6: "One or more persons may form an incorporated company under this Act." **[P]**
- **Effective date.** Date of assent and date of *Gazette* notification both **2 August 2019**. Act 992 contains no express commencement section; s.1(1)–(2) refer to "the commencement of this Act" without fixing a date, so commencement follows the default rule in the Interpretation Act, 2009 (Act 792), ss.17–18, i.e. Gazette notification. **[P]** for the assent and Gazette dates (front matter and final page of the official Assembly Press text); **[S]** for the operation of Act 792 ss.17–18, whose verbatim text could not be retrieved. Point-in-time consolidation dated `eng@2019-08-02` at GhaLII corroborates. No parliamentary passage date is asserted: Ghana's printed Acts do not record one and secondary sources conflict (2 May vs 8 May 2019).
- **Statutory minimum capital: none.** A full-text search of Act 992 for "minimum stated capital", "minimum capital" and "minimum issued" returns no hits. s.68 defines stated capital as a computed sum with no floor; s.13(n)(i) requires only that the incorporation application *state* the proposed stated capital. **[P]** (negative finding). The GHS 500 figure in circulation is the floor under the *repealed* Companies Act 1963 (Act 179), continued as Registrar's administrative practice; it is not an Act 992 requirement. **[S]**
- **Binding non-capital restriction.** s.171(1): "A company incorporated after the commencement of this Act shall have **at least two directors**, one of these directors being ordinarily resident in Ghana." s.171(2) imposes a daily penalty and s.171(3) makes directors and knowing members jointly and severally liable for debts incurred while the company trades with fewer than two directors. **[P]** *Ghana therefore created a one-**shareholder** company, not a one-**person** company;* this is why Ghana is classified as barrier-constrained in §4.3 despite a zero capital requirement.
- **Foreign participation (separate statute).** GIPC Act, 2013 (Act 865) s.28: US$200,000 for a joint venture with ≥10% Ghanaian equity, US$500,000 wholly foreign-owned, US$1,000,000 plus 20 skilled Ghanaian employees for trading. **[S]**

## Bangladesh — Companies (Second Amendment) Act, 2020 (Act No. 24 of 2020)

- **Instrument.** কোম্পানী (দ্বিতীয় সংশোধন) আইন, ২০২০, Act No. 24 of 2020. Note this is the *Second* Amendment Act; a separate Companies (Amendment) Act 2020 of February 2020 dealt with the common seal and did **not** create the OPC. **[P]**
- **Operative provisions.** s.9 of Act 24 of 2020 inserts s.2(1)(খখ) and a new Division X-A (ss.392A–392K) into the Companies Act 1994. **[P]**
- **Dates.** Passed by the Jatiya Sangsad **18 November 2020** **[S]**; published in the Bangladesh Gazette **26 November 2020** **[P]**. RJSC began accepting OPC registrations **23 May 2021** **[S]**, which is the treatment year used. (A law-firm assertion of "promulgated 22 November 2020" is unsupported by the Gazette entry and is disregarded.)
- **Statutory entry cost — the most restrictive in the sample.** s.392C(1) (ধারা ৩৯২গ): paid-up share capital **not less than BDT 2,500,000 (25 lakh) and not more than BDT 50,000,000 (5 crore)**; annual turnover of the immediately preceding financial year **not less than BDT 10,000,000 (1 crore) and not more than BDT 500,000,000 (50 crore)**. **[P]** (Bangla gazette text.) *Note the turnover ceiling is 50 crore, not the 100 crore reported in several secondary sources.*
- **Economic significance.** The BDT 2.5m paid-up floor is roughly **11 times** Bangladeshi GNI per capita in 2021, and the prior-year turnover *floor* means the form is legally unavailable to a firm that does not already exist and trade. The Bangladeshi OPC is a re-registration vehicle for established mid-sized traders, not an entry-liberalising form.
- **Other restrictions.** s.392B(2): a natural person may form **only one** OPC. s.392B(1): founder must be a natural person. s.392B(3)–(6): mandatory nominee with written consent recorded at registration. s.392E(1): the sole shareholder is the director. s.392I: shares transferable only to a single natural person. s.392K: mandatory audit; s.392J: accounts filed within 180 days. s.392C(2): conversion permitted (drafted permissively, করা যাইবে) where *both* the capital and turnover ceilings are exceeded. **[P]**
- **No later amendment.** The 2020 enacting text and the current official consolidation (generated 7 August 2026) are identical for s.392C, and Division X-A carries only the single insertion footnote referencing Act 24 of 2020 — no amendment footnote of any later date. Reports of a 2023 revision to the thresholds are not borne out. **[P]** (negative finding on the official consolidation).
- Sources: http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-print-1353/section-print-50122.html · http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-788/act-part-print-535.html · http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-788/section-50103.html · https://thefinancialexpress.com.bd/trade/one-person-company-registration-starts-sunday-1621649918

## Nigeria — Companies and Allied Matters Act, 2020 (CAMA 2020)

- **Operative provision.** s.18(2): "Notwithstanding subsection (1), **one person may form and incorporate a private company** by complying with the requirements of this Act in respect of private companies." **[P]**
- **Effective date.** Signed August 2020; CAC implementation from approximately January 2021, the treatment year used. **[S]**
- **Statutory minimum capital.** s.27(2)(a): minimum **issued** share capital "not less than N100,000.00 in the case of a private company"; reinforced by s.124(2) (no registration below the minimum) and s.124(4) (issued capital shall not at any time fall below it), with fines under s.124(5). CAMA 2020 replaced the former *authorised* share capital concept with *minimum issued* capital, so this is a genuine subscription obligation. **[P]** Approximately **9 per cent** of Nigerian GNI per capita in 2021 — the lowest positive capital requirement among the classified reforms.
- **Other restrictions — the most liberal in the sample.** s.271(1) requires two directors only for a company that is *not* a small company, so a single-member private company qualifying as small may lawfully have one director. s.394(3) defines "small company": private; turnover ≤ NGN 120,000,000; net assets ≤ NGN 60,000,000; **no member is an alien**; no government member; directors hold ≥51% of equity. s.330(1) no company secretary required; s.237(1) no AGM required for a small company or any single-shareholder company; s.402(1)–(2) audit exemption. No turnover cap on the form itself, no conversion threshold, no one-per-person rule, no notarial requirement (memorandum signed before one attesting witness and stamped as a deed, s.27(5)–(6)). **[P]** *The alien-exclusion in s.394(3) is a nationality restriction on the deregulated version of the form, not on the form itself.*
- **Retroactive extension by the courts.** *Corporate Affairs Commission v. Primetech Design and Engineering Nigeria Ltd & Anor*, Appeal No. CA/ABJ/CV/1226/2024 (Court of Appeal, Abuja Division, **10 June 2026**) (unreported), affirming *Primetech Design and Engineering Nigeria Ltd & Julius Berger Nigeria Plc v. Corporate Affairs Commission*, Suit No. FHC/ABJ/CS/665/2023 (Federal High Court, Abuja, Egwuatu J, **30 July 2024**) (unreported). Held: s.18(2) permits a single shareholder in **all** private companies, whether incorporated before or after CAMA 2020's commencement. No official report of either judgment is publicly available; the traceable sources are case notes by Banwo & Ighodalo (counsel for the respondents, 12 June 2026) and Templars (22 June 2026). **[S]** — cited as unreported with appeal numbers. *The panel Justices are not named in any available source and are not asserted here.*
- Sources: https://www.icnl.org/wp-content/uploads/Companies-and-Allied-Matters-Act-2020.pdf · https://www.cac.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2020/12/CAMA-NOTE-BOOK-FULL-VERSION.pdf · https://www.banwo-ighodalo.com/grey-matter/court-of-appeal-affirms-federal-high-court-judgment-regarding-single-shareholder-structure-for-private-companies-incorporated-pre-cama-2020-ca-abj-cv-1226-2024-corporate-affairs-commission/

## Ethiopia — Commercial Code Proclamation No. 1243/2021

- **Operative provisions.** Title Eight, Articles 534–545, introducing the one-member private limited company. **[P]**
- **Effective date.** Signed by President Sahle-Work Zewde **12 April 2021**; s.7 provides that the law takes effect on publication in the Federal Negarit Gazette. **[P]**
- **Statutory minimum capital.** Art. 535: "The capital of a one member private limited company shall **not be less than 15,000 (fifteen thousand) Ethiopian Birr**." The parallel PLC floor is Art. 496(1), unchanged from the 1960 Code — the requirement was **retained**, neither lowered nor abolished. Art. 536(2)(g) requires the incorporating declaration to state "that the capital has been **fully paid**," making this a paid-in rather than merely subscribed requirement. **[P]** Approximately **42 per cent** of Ethiopian GNI per capita in 2021.
- **Other restrictions.** Art. 536(1): the unilateral declaration of incorporation must be made **before an authority entrusted with authentication of documents** — a notarial requirement. Art. 537(1): the company may not be formed unless the nominee has separately declared acceptance before an authenticating authority; Art. 537(2): no person may be nominee for more than one such company. Art. 539(1): a one-member PLC may not establish another one-member PLC (a restriction on companies, not on natural persons — contrast Bangladesh's explicit one-per-person rule). Art. 540(2)–(5): auditor required to verify in-kind contributions, with five-year liability for overvaluation. Art. 541: a general manager is required but may be the member. Art. 538: a sole trader converting an enterprise remains jointly and severally liable for pre-conversion debts. Art. 543: seven veil-piercing grounds. No turnover ceiling, no mandatory conversion threshold, no nationality bar in the Code itself. **[P]**
- Sources: https://lawethiopiacomment.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/new-commercial-code-no-1243_2021_english.pdf · https://ethiodata.et/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/Commercial-Code-of-Ethiopia-English-Proclamation-No.-1243_2021.pdf

## Türkiye — Turkish Commercial Code No. 6102

- **Operative provisions.** Art. 338(1) (joint-stock company) and art. 573(1) (limited company): "one or more" founders, replacing the five-founder minimum of the former Code No. 6762 art. 277. Art. 338(2) requires notification to the board within seven days where shares concentrate in one hand, and registration and announcement of the sole shareholder's identity. **[P]**
- **Effective date.** 1 July 2012. **[P]**
- **Statutory minimum capital.** Limited company (the relevant comparator form): art. 580, subscribed capital **TRY 10,000**; art. 585 requires **25 per cent paid before registration** with the balance within 24 months, i.e. **TRY 2,500** in cash at the registry. Joint-stock company: art. 332, subscribed **TRY 50,000** (TRY 100,000 for a non-public company adopting the registered-capital system), with 25 per cent (**TRY 12,500**) paid before registration under art. 344. **[P]**
- **Important drafting history.** Code 6102 *as originally drafted* required the entire cash portion of limited-company capital to be paid at formation. This was amended by **Law No. 6335 of 26 June 2012 — four days before Code 6102 entered into force** — so the rule actually in force on 1 July 2012 is the 25 per cent rule. Sources that describe the Turkish 2012 requirement as full payment are reading the superseded draft. **[P]**
- **Non-capital restrictions.** Arts. 335/575: articles of association in writing with **notarized founders' signatures** (the alternative of signing before the trade-registry director was added only in 2016 by Law No. 6728). Art. 623(1): at least one **shareholder** must hold management and representation authority, so in a single-member limited company the sole member is compelled to serve as manager. Art. 574(1): maximum 50 shareholders. Art. 359(1): a joint-stock board may consist of one person, and directors need no longer be shareholders. No nationality or residency requirement. **[P]**
- **Subsequent changes not to be merged into the 2012 event.** Law No. 7099 (in force 15 March 2018) abolished the 25 per cent pre-registration payment for limited companies entirely. Presidential Decision No. 7887 (effective 1 January 2024) raised the minima to TRY 250,000 / TRY 50,000. **[S]**

## Saudi Arabia — Companies Law 2015 (Royal Decree M/3)

- **Operative provision.** Art. 154(1): «يجوز أن تُؤسَّس الشركة ذات المسؤولية المحدودة من شخص واحد، أو أن تؤول جميع حصصها لشخص واحد» — an LLC may be founded by one person, or all its shares may devolve to one person. **[S]**
- **Effective date.** 2 May 2016. **[S]**
- **Verification caveat — the weakest entry in this appendix.** The 2015 law was repealed by the Companies Law of 2022 (Royal Decree M/132, in force 19 January 2023), and every PDF now served online under the title "Saudi Companies Law" — including from idc.gov.sa and qadha.org.sa — resolves on extraction to the **2022** text. The 2015 provisions below therefore rest on two contemporaneous top-tier briefings that agree in terms (Jones Day, November 2015; Clifford Chance, December 2015), **not on the statute itself**. Marked **[S]** throughout.
- **Statutory minimum capital: none.** Neither the 2015 law nor the 1965 law it replaced imposed a minimum capital on LLCs; the 2015 law substitutes a qualitative "capital sufficiency" standard requiring capital adequate to the company's objects. **[S]**
- **Correction to a widely repeated figure.** The SAR 500,000 associated with Saudi Arabia is (i) the **joint-stock** minimum, which the 2015 law *reduced* from SAR 2,000,000, 25 per cent payable at incorporation and the balance within five years; and/or (ii) a **MISA/SAGIA foreign-investment licensing** floor imposed administratively on foreign investors above and beyond the Companies Law. It is not an LLC company-law requirement. A "SAR 100,000 under art. 154" figure circulating in Arabic business commentary is the MISA services floor misattributed to the statute; art. 154(1) contains no figure. **[S]**
- **Non-capital restrictions.** Art. 154(2): a natural person **may not own more than one single-shareholder LLC**, and no single-shareholder LLC may itself own another — the most binding one-per-person rule in the sample after Bangladesh's. Single-shareholder *joint-stock* companies are gated to government entities or companies with capital of at least SAR 5 million. LLC maximum 50 partners. Losses reaching 50 per cent of capital trigger a mandatory dissolution procedure. Foreign founders remain subject to MISA/SAGIA licensing, the binding entry constraint for non-GCC founders. **[S]**
- Sources: https://www.jonesday.com/-/media/files/publications/2015/11/saudi-arabia-new-companies-law-2015-approved/files/saudi-arabia-new-companies-law-2015-approved/fileattachment/saudi-arabia-new-companies-law-2015.pdf · https://www.ashcliffordchance.com/content/dam/cliffordchance/briefings/2015/12/saudi-arabia-new-companies-law.pdf

## Malaysia — Companies Act 2016 (Act 777)

- **Operative provision.** s.9: "A company shall have— (a) a name; (b) **one or more members**…; (c) in the case of a company limited by shares, **one or more shares**; and (d) **one or more directors**." **[P]**
- **Effective date.** 31 January 2017. **[P]**
- **Statutory minimum capital: none.** A full-text search of the gazetted Act and the 1 August 2022 consolidated reprint returns zero occurrences of "minimum capital", "minimum paid-up", "authorised capital" or "nominal capital". The only capital requirement is at least one share (s.9(c)); s.74 abolishes par value; s.14(3)(h) requires the incorporation application to state only the class and number of shares, not an amount. Authorised capital is abolished by omission — no section repeals it, and s.618 concerns transitional treatment of nominal value only. **[P]** (negative finding). *The "RM1 minimum" is a practitioner formulation appearing in no statutory or SSM document; cite the sections, not the figure.*
- **Where the Malaysian entry cost actually lies — a finding that matters for §4.3.** With a genuine zero capital floor, the binding statutory entry costs are non-monetary: s.196(4) requires the **minimum number of directors to ordinarily reside in Malaysia** with a principal place of residence there; s.235(1) requires at least one **company secretary who is a Malaysian citizen or permanent resident**, ordinarily resident in Malaysia, and either a member of a Fourth Schedule body or SSM-licensed, appointed within 30 days of incorporation (s.236(2)); s.267(1) makes audit the default obligation, and the exemption regime did **not yet operate at the reform date** — SSM Practice Directive 3/2017 issued 4 August 2017 and staged the exemption between August 2017 and July 2018. **[P]** Maximum 50 shareholders (s.42(1)); constitution optional (s.31(1)); no notarial deed; incorporation is a wholly electronic filing via MyCoID 2016.
- **Fees.** Companies Regulations 2017 [P.U.(A) 37], gazetted 26 January 2017 and in operation 31 January 2017, Schedule item 1: incorporation of a company limited by shares **RM1,000**; item 3: name reservation RM50 per 30 days. **[P]** *The commonly cited "RM1,010" appears in no primary source.*
- Sources: https://lom.agc.gov.my/ilims/upload/portal/akta/outputaktap/aktaBI_20160915_CompaniesAct2016Act777.pdf · https://www.ssm.com.my/Pages/Legal_Framework/Document/Companies%20Act%202016_Akta%20777_BI%20(1.8.2022).pdf · https://www.ssm.com.my/Pages/About_SSM/PDF/company_regulation_pua_20170126_p_u_a_37_0.pdf

## Egypt — Law No. 4 of 2018 amending Law No. 159/1981

- **Operative provision.** New art. 129 *mukarrar* of Law 159/1981, granting every natural or legal person the right to found a one-person company (شركة الشخص الواحد). **[P]**
- **Effective date.** Published 16 January 2018. **[P]** (Gazette issue number not obtained.)
- **Statutory minimum capital: EGP 50,000, fully paid at incorporation — but the figure is regulatory, not statutory.** Law 4/2018 (art. 129 *mukarrar*-1) imposes only the full-payment rule and delegates the amount. The number is set by **art. 287 *mukarrar*-2 of the Executive Regulations (Decree 96/1982), inserted by Minister of Investment Decree No. 16/2018**: «لا يجوز أن يقل الحد الأدنى لرأس مال شركة الشخص الواحد عن خمسين ألف جنيه ويجب أن يدفع رأس المال بالكامل عند تأسيس الشركة». No instalments are permitted. **[P]** via two independent renderings of the decree; the government PDF at sis.gov.eg returns an SSL error and was not fetchable, so this is one step short of gazette-grade. *Citing "Law 4/2018" for the EGP 50,000 figure is a citation error and should be avoided.*
- **Magnitude.** EGP 50,000 is approximately **1.16 times** Egyptian GNI per capita in 2018, fully paid in cash at incorporation — placing Egypt among the high-barrier reforms, not the low-barrier ones.
- **Non-capital restrictions.** Art. 129 *mukarrar*-2: an OPC may not found another OPC; public subscription, tradeable shares and tradeable debt securities prohibited; banking, insurance, deposit-taking and third-party fund management excluded. Mandatory auditor. Art. 4 *mukarrar*: the name must disclose OPC status on premises, branches and all correspondence. Art. 129 *mukarrar*-9: the company dissolves on the owner's death unless it passes to a single heir or the heirs regularize within six months. Art. 129 *mukarrar*-5: partial transfer of ownership requires conversion to another form within 90 days. Signatures must be authenticated by the Real Estate Registration and Notarization Department. **[P]**
- **A restriction Egypt does *not* have.** No prohibition on a natural person owning several OPCs appears in either the Law or the Regulations; the ban runs only against an *OPC* founding another OPC. Secondary literature routinely asserts a one-per-person rule for Egypt by analogy with Saudi Arabia's art. 154(2); it is not supported by the text and is not asserted here. **[P]** (negative finding).
- **Procedural cost — the offsetting liberalization.** GAFI operates a one-stop shop with a stated **one-business-day** service level, completing Bar Association endorsement, FRA approval, Chamber of Commerce membership, Commercial Register entry, tax card and insurance account on the applicant's behalf. GAFI's own worked example totals approximately **EGP 599** in fees. **[P]** *This matters for interpretation: Egypt combined a high capital floor with a drastic cut in procedural cost, and did so alongside Investment Law No. 72/2017 — see §4.4.*
- **Subsequent change (outside the sample window).** Prime Ministerial Decree No. 2928/2022 (21 August 2022) cut the floor from EGP 50,000 to **EGP 1,000**, retaining full payment. **[S]**

## Philippines — Republic Act No. 11232, Revised Corporation Code

- **Operative provisions.** s. 10 replaces the former requirement of "not less than five (5)" natural persons (Batas Pambansa Blg. 68, s. 10) with permission to incorporate "singly or jointly with others," and provides that "[a] corporation with a single stockholder is considered a One Person Corporation." Title XIII, Chapter III (ss. 115–132) governs the form. **[P]**
- **Dates.** Approved 20 February 2019; effective 23 February 2019 on completion of publication. SEC began accepting OPC applications **6 May 2019** following approval of MC 7-2019 on 25 April; the first OPC was incorporated 7 May 2019. **[P]** (SEC press release).
- **Statutory minimum capital: none.** s. 117: "A One Person Corporation shall not be required to have a minimum authorized capital stock except as otherwise provided by special law"; s. 12 says the same for stock corporations generally. SEC MC 7-2019 §8: "no portion of the authorized capital is required to be paid-up at the time of incorporation." **[P]** *Note the former 25 per cent subscribed / 25 per cent paid-in rule of BP 68 s. 13 was not repealed by s. 12 — it was simply not carried into the new Code, and survives only in s. 37 for increases of capital stock.*
- **The bond — a real entry cost the "no minimum capital" label conceals.** s. 122: a single stockholder who is also self-appointed treasurer "shall give a bond to the Commission in such a sum as may be required," renewable every two years. SEC MC 7-2019 §10 sets the schedule: authorised capital up to ₱1,000,000 requires bond coverage of **₱1,000,000**, rising in steps to ₱5,000,000, and above ₱5,000,000 coverage equal to the authorised capital. **[S]** (reproduction of a primary instrument; SEC's own PDF returns 403). *The bond has never been repealed; SEC MC 10-2026 retains it while permitting surety, cash or property bonds and adding a formal release procedure where a separate treasurer is appointed.* Because it is avoidable by appointing another treasurer, it is coded as a non-capital restriction rather than a capital requirement.
- **Other restrictions.** s. 116: only a natural person, trust or estate may form an OPC; banks and quasi-banks, preneed, trust, insurance, public and publicly listed companies and non-chartered GOCCs are excluded; a licensed professional may not organise an OPC to practise that profession. ss. 118(b), 124–125: a nominee **and** an alternate nominee must be named in the articles with written consent. s. 121: the single stockholder is sole director and president; s. 122: but may not be corporate secretary. **[P]**
- Sources: https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2019/ra_11232_2019.html · https://appointment.sec.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/2019PressRelease_SEC_registers_country_first_one_person_corporation.pdf

## Uruguay — Ley 19.820, Sociedad por Acciones Simplificada

- **Operative provision.** Art. 11: "La sociedad por acciones simplificada podrá constituirse por una persona física, una persona jurídica distinta de una sociedad anónima o varias personas físicas o jurídicas." Art. 21: while the company has a single shareholder, that shareholder may exercise the powers of all corporate organs. **[P]**
- **Dates.** Promulgated 18 September 2019, published in the *Diario Oficial* 27 September 2019; Decreto 399/019 of 23 December 2019 published 7 January 2020; the regime is coded as operational from 2020. **[P]**
- **Statutory minimum capital: none.** Art. 15 fixes no floor: capital must be fully subscribed at constitution, with **at least 10 per cent paid in if in cash** (100 per cent if in kind) and full integration within 24 months. Art. 12(F) requires only that the figure be stated. **[P]**
- **Corrections to widely repeated claims.** (i) The bar in art. 11 is on a *sociedad anónima* being the sole founder — there is no Uruguayan "SAU" (that is Argentina), and nothing prevents a SAS from being the sole shareholder of another SAS. (ii) The UI 37,500,000 annual-income figure in art. 10 is real but does **not** force conversion into an SA: it triggers automatic supervision by the state control organ after 180 days, on the terms applicable to closed SAs, with the company remaining a SAS. **[P]**
- **Electronic incorporation post-dates the reform.** Art. 11 authorises fully web-based constitution with advanced electronic signature, but Decreto 399/019 art. 3 imposed a transitional paper procedure at the Registro Nacional de Comercio pending the AGESIC platform, which went live only on **22 July 2022**. Descriptions of Uruguayan SAS incorporation as online and notary-free are therefore accurate for 2022 onward, not for the 2020–21 treatment years. **[P]/[S]**
- **No prior one-member company.** Ley 16.060 art. 1 requires "dos o más personas"; the pre-existing *empresa unipersonal* was a sole proprietorship without separate legal personality. The SAS is Uruguay's first one-member company with legal personality. **[P]**

## Ecuador — Ley Orgánica de Emprendimiento e Innovación, Sociedad por Acciones Simplificada

- **Operative provision.** The SAS was inserted by LOEI *Disposición Reformatoria Octava* as an **unnumbered** *sección* after Sección VIII of the Ley de Compañías, composed of unnumbered articles; it must be cited by rubric, not article number. The *Limitación de responsabilidad* article provides that the SAS "podrá constituirse por una o varias personas naturales o jurídicas." **[P]**
- **Date.** *Registro Oficial* Suplemento No. 151, **28 February 2020**. **[P]**
- **Statutory minimum capital: none — the cleanest zero in the sample.** The unnumbered article *Valor nominal y capital mínimo*: shares have a nominal value of one US dollar or multiples, and "La sociedad por acciones simplificada **no tendrá un requerimiento de capital mínimo**." **[P]**
- **Procedure.** Incorporation by private document ("documento privado") registered in the Registro de Sociedades of the Superintendencia de Compañías, Valores y Seguros — displacing both the public deed and the Registro Mercantil — and expressly available electronically; a public deed is required only where contributed assets themselves require one. The official SCVS trámite sheet states "El trámite no tiene costo." No statutory processing timeframe was located and none is asserted. **[P]**
- **Other restrictions.** Barred from financial, securities-market and insurance operations and others with special statutory treatment; shares may not be listed in the Catastro Público de Mercado de Valores or exchange-traded; cash capital payable within 24 months, in-kind contributions 100 per cent at subscription. **[P]** *(The Ley Orgánica de Solidaridad Nacional of June 2025 widened the prohibitions, but the Corte Constitucional annulled that law in its entirety for formal unconstitutionality in October 2025, restoring the 2020 wording; some codifications still display the annulled text.)*

## Indonesia — Perseroan Perorangan (UU 11/2020; PP 8/2021)

- **Operative provisions.** UU 40/2007 arts. 153A–153J, inserted by UU 11/2020 art. 109; implementing regulation PP 8/2021. Art. 153A(2): incorporation rests on a *surat pernyataan pendirian* (statement of establishment) in Indonesian — **no notarial deed**; a notarial deed is required only to convert *out* of the form (PP 8/2021 art. 9(2)). **[P]**
- **Date.** Registration portal opened 8 October 2021 **[S]**; 2022 is coded as the first full treated year.
- **Statutory minimum capital: no nominal floor, but not "no capital rule."** UU 40/2007 art. 32 as amended: the *modal dasar* is "ditentukan berdasarkan keputusan pendiri Perseroan" — the IDR 50,000,000 floor for a PT is repealed. However **PP 8/2021 art. 4(1) requires 25 per cent of the founder-chosen modal dasar to be placed and fully paid**, evidenced electronically within 60 days, and art. 5 preserves sectoral minimums. The effective floor is therefore zero, since the founder chooses the base. **[P]**
- **Eligibility and other restrictions.** Confined to micro and small enterprises as defined by PP 7/2021 art. 35 — micro: business capital up to IDR 1,000,000,000 excluding land and buildings, annual sales up to IDR 2,000,000,000; small: capital above IDR 1bn to IDR 5,000,000,000, sales above IDR 2bn to IDR 15,000,000,000; the *capital* criterion governs at incorporation. These are ceilings, not floors, and so do not bar entry. Founder must be an Indonesian citizen, at least 17 years old and legally competent (PP 8/2021 art. 6). **One such company per person per year — UU 40/2007 art. 153E(2), a statutory rule, not PP 8/2021 art. 7.** Registration fee **IDR 50,000** (PMK 49/PMK.02/2021, effective 19 May 2021), unchanged since. **[P]**

## India — Companies Act 2013, s. 2(62), One Person Company

- **Operative provisions.** s. 2(62) defines the OPC as "a company which has only one person as a member"; s. 3(1)(c) permits formation by "one person, where the company to be formed is to be One Person Company that is to say, a private company." Companies (Incorporation) Rules 2014, G.S.R. 250(E) of 31 March 2014. **[P]**
- **Effective date.** 1 April 2014 (S.O. 902(E), Sl. No. 16). *Citation note: MCA's own PDF of the 26 March 2014 notification is a pre-gazette signed copy with a blank S.O. number; the number 902(E) comes from India Code's commencement footnote to s. 1, so cite both together.* **[P]**
- **Statutory minimum capital at the reform: ₹100,000 — and the enforcement mechanism is the interesting part.** Because an OPC is by definition a private company, the ₹1 lakh minimum paid-up share capital in s. 2(68) applied to it via s. 3(1)(c). Registration itself did not verify paid-up capital; the requirement was enforced instead by **original s. 11(1)(a)**, which barred a company from commencing business or exercising borrowing powers until a director filed a declaration (e-Form INC-21) that subscribers had paid up and that paid-up capital was "not less than one lakh rupees in case of a private company," with the Registrar empowered under s. 11(3) to strike the company off after 180 days. The Indian OPC of 2014 could therefore be *incorporated* empty but could not lawfully *trade or borrow* until ₹1 lakh was actually paid in. **[P]**
- **Duration — material to the coding.** The floor lasted **thirteen months**: the Companies (Amendment) Act 2015 (Act 21 of 2015) deleted the words in s. 2(68) by §2 and omitted s. 11 entirely by §4, both commenced **29 May 2015** by S.O. 1440(E). The barrier coding records the requirement in force at the reform date, with a robustness recoding India to zero (§4.3).
- **Non-capital restrictions as originally enacted.** Rule 3(1): only "a natural person who is an Indian citizen and resident in India," residence defined as at least 182 days in the immediately preceding **calendar** year (changed to "financial year" only in 2018 by G.S.R. 708(E); the 182-day test became 120 days in 2021). Rule 3(2): no person may incorporate more than one OPC or be nominee in more than one. Mandatory nominee named in the memorandum (s. 3(1), first proviso; Forms INC-2/INC-3/INC-4). Rule 3(6): no non-banking financial investment activity. **Rule 6(1)** (not Rule 3): mandatory conversion where paid-up capital exceeds ₹50 lakh **or** average annual turnover over the preceding three consecutive financial years exceeds ₹2 crore — the conjunction was changed from "or" to "and" by G.S.R. 349(E) of 1 May 2015, so the disjunctive form governed only the first thirteen months. Rule 3(7): no voluntary conversion for two years. All of these were swept away by the Companies (Incorporation) Second Amendment Rules 2021, G.S.R. 91(E) of 1 February 2021, in force 1 April 2021, which also opened the form to non-resident Indian citizens. **[P]**
- **Volumes.** OPC registrations rose from 2,223 in FY2014-15 to 4,843 (FY2016-17), 6,602 (FY2018-19) and 8,949 (FY2020-21), holding steady at 4.6–6.2 per cent of all company incorporations throughout — growth in step with aggregate incorporation rather than a widening share. Source: MCA, *Monthly Information Bulletin on Corporate Sector*, March issues. **[P]** *(Figures of 20,000–36,000 that circulate in the literature are the cumulative stock on the register, not annual flow.)*

## Brazil — Lei 12.441/2011 (EIRELI) and Lei 13.874/2019 (single-member *limitada*)

- **Operative provision.** Civil Code art. 980-A, inserted by Lei 12.441/2011: "A empresa individual de responsabilidade limitada será constituída por uma única pessoa titular da totalidade do capital social, **devidamente integralizado**, que não será inferior a **100 (cem) vezes o maior salário-mínimo vigente no País**." **[P]**
- **Effective date.** Art. 3º provides entry into force 180 days after publication (12 July 2011), giving 8–9 January 2012; 9 January is commonly cited. The exact day is **[S]**.
- **Statutory minimum capital: R$62,200 (2012), fully paid in at registration.** "Devidamente integralizado" means fully paid up, not merely subscribed. The multiplicand is the federal monthly minimum wage in force on 1 January 2012, **R$622.00**, set by **Decreto nº 7.655 of 23 December 2011** (DOU 26 December 2011), art. 1º, with art. 2º fixing entry into force at 1 January 2012. The 2012 decree took effect days before the EIRELI law did, so R$622 — not the 2011 figure of R$545 — is the correct multiplicand. **[P]** R$62,200 is approximately **2.6 times** Brazilian GNI per capita in 2012, by far the heaviest capital requirement in the sample outside Bangladesh.
- **Other restrictions.** Art. 980-A §2º: a natural person may hold only one EIRELI. "EIRELI" must appear in the firm name. Registration at the Junta Comercial with no notarial deed; no turnover ceiling and no director minimum — the capital floor is essentially the entire barrier, which is what makes Brazil the cleanest single-country test in the sample. **[P]**
- **The 2019 second liberalization.** Lei 13.874/2019 (Lei da Liberdade Econômica), art. 7º, inserted Civil Code art. 1.052 §§1–2 permitting a *sociedade limitada* to be formed by one or more persons, **with no minimum capital**. Effective on publication, 20 September 2019. **[S]** (Planalto blocked automated retrieval throughout; verified at search level only.) This is the basis of the alternative 2019 coding reported in Table 3.

## Argentina — Sociedad Anónima Unipersonal (Ley 26.994) and SAS (Ley 27.349)

- **Operative provision.** Ley General de Sociedades art. 1, as amended by Ley 26.994 in force 1 August 2015: "**La sociedad unipersonal sólo se podrá constituir como sociedad anónima.** La sociedad unipersonal no puede constituirse por una sociedad unipersonal." **[P]**
- **Statutory minimum capital: ARS 100,000, integrated in full at incorporation.** Because the SAU could only take the *sociedad anónima* form, it carried the art. 186 minimum capital, fixed at **ARS 100,000** by **Decreto 1331/2012 art. 1º** (B.O. 7 August 2012). Art. 187 provides that cash integration may not be less than 25 per cent of subscription "**En la Sociedad Anónima Unipersonal el capital social deberá estar totalmente integrado**," reinforced by art. 186 inc. 3. So the day-one cash requirement was ARS 100,000, against ARS 25,000 for an ordinary SA. **[P]** Approximately **0.74 times** Argentine GNI per capita in 2015.
- **The governance burden — why the manuscript's "low barrier" label is most wrong here.** Art. 299 inc. 7 placed SAUs under **permanent state supervision** from 1 August 2015 (footnoted in the consolidated text to Ley 26.994, *Vigencia: 1° de agosto de 2015*, and never removed). Consequently, between **1 August 2015 and 17 November 2016**, a SAU required a **plural directorate of at least three directors** (art. 255) and a **collegiate sindicatura of three** (art. 284). The carve-outs now visible in both articles ("salvo en las previstas en el inciso 7)"; "excepto en los casos previstos en los incisos 2 y 7") were introduced by **Ley 27.290** (B.O. 18 November 2016), arts. 1 and 2. **[P]** — established by construction from two retrieved primary texts, the consolidated LGS with its amendment footnotes and Ley 27.290 itself. *The 2015 SAU was therefore substantially more burdensome than an ordinary SRL, which had no minimum capital, 25 per cent cash integration with two years to complete, a single gerente and optional sindicatura.*
- **The 2017 second liberalization.** Ley 27.349 (B.O. 12 April 2017) created the SAS: art. 40 sets minimum capital at "el importe equivalente a **dos (2) veces el salario mínimo vital y móvil**," and art. 41 restores the ordinary 25 per cent at subscription with the balance within two years. The SMVM was **ARS 8,060** from 1 January 2017 (Resolución 2/2016 CNEPySMVM art. 1), so 2× = **ARS 16,120**, of which only about **ARS 4,030** was due at incorporation. Art. 34 permits a single shareholder but bars a unipersonal SAS from constituting or participating in another. The law has no commencement clause, so 20 April 2017 is **derived** from CCyC art. 5, not stated. **[P]**

## Mexico — Sociedad por Acciones Simplificada (LGSM reform)

- **Operative provision.** LGSM art. 260 ¶1, as added by the decree published in the *Diario Oficial de la Federación* on 14 March 2016: the SAS "se constituye con **una o más personas físicas**." **[P]**
- **Effective date.** The decree's transitorio provides entry into force six months from the day after publication, giving **15 September 2016**. **[P]**
- **Statutory minimum capital: none.** No capital floor appears anywhere in the SAS articles of the decree. There is likewise no statutory "one peso" rule; the LGSM has set no minimum for the S.A. or S. de R.L. since the 2011 reform. **[P]** (verified by absence).
- **Other restrictions.** Art. 260 ¶1, second sentence: a natural person may not simultaneously be a shareholder of another mercantile company of the types in art. 1º fracs. I–VII "si su participación en dichas sociedades mercantiles les permite tener el control de la sociedad o de su administración." Art. 260 also imposes an **annual income ceiling of MXN 5,000,000**, above which the company must transform into another corporate form; the ceiling is indexed by reference to art. 17-A of the Código Fiscal de la Federación, with the Secretaría de Economía publishing the factor annually. Being a ceiling rather than a floor, it does not bar entry. No notarial deed is required (art. 262, final paragraph) and incorporation is electronic through gob.mx/tuempresa (art. 263) — both **[S]**. **[P]** on capital, ceiling and effective date.
- *Free incorporation is widely reported but has no statutory basis: the word "gratuito" does not appear in the SAS articles, and no primary source for the administrative fee waiver was located. It is not asserted here.* **[N]**

## Excluded on substantive grounds — Switzerland (revision of 16 December 2005, in force 1 January 2008)

*Not a Table 1 entry. Recorded here because the coding table classifies Switzerland as an in-window statutory reform and the paper (§3.3) excludes it from both the treated set and the comparison pool; a reader checking the replication package will see the discrepancy.*

- **Instrument.** Bundesgesetz vom 16. Dezember 2005 (GmbH-Recht sowie Anpassungen im Aktien-, Genossenschafts-, Handelsregister- und Firmenrecht), amending the Code of Obligations. **Entry into force 1 January 2008.** **[P]** — WIPO Lex record of the Federal Act, which states both the adoption date and the 1 January 2008 commencement.
- **What the revision changed.** It removed the minimum-founder requirements: three founding members for the *Aktiengesellschaft* and two members for the *GmbH*, after which both forms may be established by a single natural or legal person. **[S]** — the three-founder rule for the AG is stated by the University of Zurich company-law teaching materials (Vogt chair); the two-member rule for the GmbH by general secondary sources. The pre-revision article text (Art. 625, Art. 775 aOR) could not be retrieved from Fedlex, which serves point-in-time versions only through a JavaScript interface; **no pre-revision provision is quoted here.** **[N]** for verbatim statutory text.
- **Why this is not a legalization event in the sense of Table 1.** The Swiss Federal Supreme Court had recognized the *Einmanngesellschaft* as retaining full legal personality since **BGE 81 II 455 (1955)**, affirmed in BGE 85 II 111 (1959) (a company's single-member character does not by itself make invocation of its separate personality abusive). Sole ownership of a Swiss limited liability entity was therefore available, and judicially protected, half a century before the sample window opens. What the 2005 revision lifted was a formality of the founding act — the need to present additional subscribers at incorporation — not a prohibition on solo ownership. This is the same distinction on which Qatar was excluded (form extant since Law 5/2002; the 2015 statute removed its capital floor). **[P]** for the two BGE decisions.
- **Independent coverage grounds.** Even taking 2008 at face value, the World Bank series for Switzerland begins in 2006, leaving two pre-reform years (2006–2007). The series also falls from 26,347 registrations in 2019 to 15,053 in 2020 and does not recover by 2022, a break of a magnitude not seen in comparable high-income series over the same years; its cause was not established, which is a further reason not to use Switzerland as a comparison unit spanning the COVID period. **[N]** on the cause of the break.
- **Effect on estimates: none.** Switzerland appears in neither the treated list nor the comparison pool constructed in `cs_estimation.py`, before or after the recoding. The estimation sample was re-derived from the amended coding table on 7 August 2026 and is identical to the one reported in §3.3 — the same 16 treated and 50 comparison economies, the same 1,162 country-years — so every reported figure is unchanged by construction.
